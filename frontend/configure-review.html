Set up the search

Tell the platform where on the protein to look, which docking program to use, and which molecules to test. Everything you choose is saved with the campaign so the result can be audited later.

1

Binding site

The binding site is the pocket on the protein where a drug would sit. The most reliable way to define it is to reuse the pocket of a molecule that was already captured in the experimental structure.

Enter the centre and edge lengths of a search box in ångström (Å), in the same coordinate frame as the structure file. Manual boxes work with AutoDock Vina only, because HYDE and FlexX (also when run on HPSee) need a reference molecule to find the pocket.

No ligand was found in this structure, so the site must be a manual box (Vina only, no HYDE re-scoring).
2

Docking program

Docking tries many ways of fitting each molecule into the pocket and scores the best fit. The two programs use different scoring scales, so their numbers cannot be compared with each other.

3

Chemical space

A chemical space is a recipe book (building blocks plus reactions) describing far more molecules than could ever be listed. HPSee never enumerates it. Anchoring first docks and scores the building blocks on their own. You then review the best anchors and choose which to grow. Extension builds only those anchors into full molecules of the space and docks them. Spaces with three building blocks need a second review and extension round.

SeeSAR definition files required

HPSee's Chemical Space Docking takes the protein, pocket and docking/scoring settings from two files exported from SeeSAR's Docking mode: a docking definition (.flexx) and a scoring definition (.hydescorer). They cannot be created here: the format belongs to BioSolveIT and is not documented. Export them from the same structure you chose above, because HPSee uses the protein inside these files, not this page's.

Filters are passed to HPSee unchanged, in its own syntax: Properties.<NAME> <op> <value>, combined with AND/OR and parentheses; operators = != < > <= >=. The anchoring filter decides which building blocks are docked; the extension filter is applied to the grown molecules. "Best products" is how many complete products, ranked by HYDE's estimated affinity, go on to synthetic-accessibility and ADMET screening.

3

Molecules to test

Molecules are fetched automatically from ZINC, a public catalogue of purchasable compounds. These are existing catalogue molecules, not newly invented ones.

Sampling seed: the molecules are a sample, picked by a pseudo-random draw. The seed is the number that starts that draw: the same seed always gives the same molecules, so a result can be reproduced and audited; a different seed gives a different sample. (It does not change how docking is scored.)

Every candidate molecule is checked against these before docking, regardless of source. Molecules outside any range are dropped during the library step (see Removed by a rule after the run for exact counts). Not available here: ZINC's own site can also filter by purchasability tier, pH state and net charge — those need live ZINC catalogue/vendor data our offline SMILES download does not carry, so they cannot be offered honestly.

⚠ Measured directly: the ZINC20 archive this app streams from contains almost no fragment-sized molecules (every chunk checked clustered around 16-31 heavy atoms) — the fragment-like preset will likely keep zero molecules when the source is ZINC20. It works normally against the built-in drug list or your own uploaded molecules.

4

Screening steps and filters

Each step removes molecules for a stated reason. Scores are never blended into one number.

Docking settings

HYDE re-scoring BioSolveIT

A second, physics-based opinion on each pose that accounts for the energy cost of removing water. It improves the ranking.

Synthetic accessibility

Scale 1 (easy to make) to 10 (very hard). Molecules above the limit are removed.

ADMET safety screen ADMET-AI model

Predicts how the body may absorb, process and tolerate each molecule. A molecule gets an alert when its predicted risk (mutagenicity, heart-channel block, liver injury, clinical toxicity or carcinogenicity) is both likely and higher than 90% of approved drugs.

Rank the survivors by

Only this one measure orders the list. The others are shown beside it.

Suggest new analogues FastGrow

After ranking, FastGrow grows small fragments from the best candidates into free space in the pocket and proposes new molecules. These are computer-generated suggestions, listed separately: they are not catalogue compounds, have not been made, and their score is not an affinity.

Binding affinity and unbinding kinetics (Koffee) placeholder

The Koffee tools are not connected yet. If left on, these steps add clearly marked placeholder values that never affect which molecules pass or how they are ranked.
